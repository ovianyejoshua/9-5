The reason your salary stays the same no matter how hard you work is not about you.

It is not your performance or your attitude. It is not that you haven't found the right company yet or that you need to be more patient or that your time is coming.

It is your container.

What is a container?

Your container is the structure your work sits inside. It is how your job is arranged to pay you. And it is this structure — not your skill, not your effort, not your excellence — that determines how much you can ever earn before you even begin.

For example

Two people leave university with the same degree. Both go into finance. One joins a bank as a staff member. He has a fixed salary, fixed increment, and fixed career ladder. He works hard, gets promoted, but he can't earn past a certain amount. That's his ceiling.

The other one eventually moves into financial consulting. They charge clients directly for advice that saves or makes those clients significant money. They charge the clients a percentage for the outcome. His income has no ceiling. He can make what the first person earns monthly in a week.

What's the difference between them? It is clearly not hard work, or their grade, or how smart they are. The difference is where they both work hard in. The structure. The container. How their work is arranged to pay them.
