It's structure. I call it a container — but it's just structure.

The reason you earn what you earn.

Some jobs are designed in a way that allows the people inside them to earn more than others ever could. Same degree. Same work ethic. Completely different ceiling.

For example

Two people leave university with the same degree. Both go into finance. One joins a bank as a staff member — fixed salary, fixed increment, fixed career ladder. He works hard. Gets promoted. But he cannot earn past a certain amount. That's his ceiling.

The other eventually moves into financial consulting. He charges clients directly for advice that saves or makes them significant money. He charges a percentage of the outcome. His income has no ceiling. He can make in one week what the first person earns in a month.

What's the difference? Not hard work. Not grades. Not intelligence. The difference is the structure they're both working hard inside. The container.

That is the reason you earn what you earn.

Let's make this personal. If your company made an extra 5 million naira in revenue this month — would you benefit from it? Would your salary change to reflect that?

If your answer is no, you are inside a container.

A structure designed to pay you not what you deserve, but what someone above you — HR, a manager, a salary band written before you ever arrived — decided your role is worth. Your performance moves you within that band. But the band itself was decided before you showed up.

If you're thinking the solution is just changing jobs or working harder — you're not yet seeing it. A better job is usually the same container with a slightly higher ceiling. A side hustle built on the same time-for-money logic hits its own ceiling fast. Working harder inside a structure that was never designed to reward harder work just makes you better at something that doesn't pay you more for being better.
